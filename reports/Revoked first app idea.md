# Build the agency handover app before anything else

The first app to build on revoked should be a **client-access handover tool for DACH web agencies, freelancers and small IT service providers**. An agency opens a client project. The tool sends the client one signed revoked request for domain, DNS, hosting, CMS and API credentials. The client answers from their own vault, so the agency always reads the *current* password. When the engagement ends, one click revokes every grant and produces a rotation checklist. Of all the candidates in the notes, this is the only one where all three of revoked's distinctive properties do real work: the living value, owner-controlled revocation and a DNS-verified requester. It is also the one with proven willingness to pay (Leadsie charges $59/month for three onboardings, and it covers only platforms with partner APIs), and the one where the client, not just the requester, gains something that makes the account requirement worth it. The strongest alternate is an **e-invoicing master-data bridge** that keeps customers' billing data, electronic delivery address and Leitweg-ID current in Invoice Ninja and Lexware Office before the 2027/2028 German issuing mandate. It has better timing, but for now it lacks a reason for the customer to sign up. The second alternate is an **n8n community node**, which is the cheapest way to put revoked in front of a large self-hosted audience. Before any of them ships, revoked's integration surface needs work. A scoped API key cannot yet read request responses. Callbacks are unsigned, fire once and are never retried. Every third-party integrator hits both walls on day one.

## Only credential handoff passes all four tests

The candidates split cleanly once you ask whether revoked's *distinctive* mechanics carry the product, or merely decorate it. Consumer personal data stores keep failing on exactly this point. A 2023 review found that **no personal data store has achieved significant mass-market penetration**, and it named the two-sided adoption problem as the main barrier ([Fallatah et al., *Sensors* 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/3/1477)). Plaxo proved in the 2000s that "update once, propagate everywhere" can reach **about 20M users**, but it grew through contact e-mails that drew a years-long spam backlash, and it shut down in 2017 once address books moved into phones and social networks ([Wikipedia: Plaxo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaxo); [Techdirt](https://techdirt.com/articles/20060322/0317223.shtml)). A first app therefore needs a requester who is motivated to pull responders in, and a responder who gains something personally.

| Candidate | (a) Painful, frequent | (b) Living grant / revocation / identity is the product | (c) Solo, in weeks | (d) Path to first users |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Agency/contractor credential handoff** | High: every onboarding and offboarding | All three are load-bearing | High: `domain_handover`, `server_access`, `api_credentials` templates already ship | Good: proven $59–258/mo tool budgets; the client has a stake |
| B2B master data / e-invoicing | Medium–high: a deadline wave in 2027/28, then event-driven | Liveness is core, identity is strong, revocation is weak | High: Lexware and Invoice Ninja APIs exist | Weak at first: the responder gains little until peers use it |
| n8n node | Channel, not a problem | Amplifies whatever it carries | Highest | Best channel: instant npm publish |
| Self-updating contact card | Low | Liveness and revocation are core | Highest: vCard/CardDAV is already built | Good for stars, poor for revenue |
| Change-of-address propagation | Real but rare per person | Liveness is core | Medium | Poor: consumer network; Deutsche Post already reaches 1,000+ firms |
| Event/trip data collection | Seasonal | Expiry fits storage limitation | High | Poor: volunteer budgets; an account is a hard sell against Google Forms |
| "Fill with Revoked" SDK | Depends on host | Weak until a network exists | Low: extension review can take weeks | Poor: needs a network that already exists |

Credential handoff wins because the pain is recurrent and the gap is specific. The tools agencies buy today fall into three families, and none does the whole job. Partner-access tools such as Leadsie cover **31 account types across Meta, Google and 14 more platforms**, and nothing else ([Leadsie](https://www.leadsie.com/)). Form collectors such as Content Snare ($35–258/month) store answers as the requester's copy ([Portico](https://www.portico.run/blog/post/content-snare-pricing)). One-time-secret links move a value but carry no request, template or verified requester. The long tail with no delegation API is WordPress admin, hosting, registrar, DNS, SMTP, SSH and SaaS API keys, and it still travels by e-mail. Upwork's official advice for an agency that should not see production keys is to "make a second key", a manual workaround rather than a product ([Upwork Help](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015855747-How-API-key-ownership-and-sharing-works-on-Upwork)).

The offboarding half is where revoked's mechanics become the product. **Third-party involvement in breaches doubled from 15% to 30%** in Verizon's 2025 DBIR ([Verizon](https://www.verizon.com/about/news/2025-data-breach-investigations-report)). That category also includes supply-chain compromise, so it must not be pitched as "30% of breaches are un-revoked freelancer logins". A 2025 Ponemon study whose sample included Germany found **47% of organisations had a breach involving third-party network access in the past year**, and only 34% keep a full inventory of their third-party relationships ([Ponemon-Sullivan](https://ponemonsullivanreport.com/2025/03/creating-a-cybersecurity-infrastructure-to-reduce-third-party-and-privileged-internal-access-risks-a-global-study/)). Among workers who kept using a former employer's passwords, **58% said the passwords were simply never changed** (an older survey; [PasswordManager.com](https://www.passwordmanager.com/47-of-workers-admit-to-hacking-accounts-with-former-employers-passwords/)). A living grant turns "remember to rotate" into the default path. When the client rotates, the current agency's grant resolves to the new value, and the departed agency's revoked grant resolves to nothing. On the requesting side, fake data requests are an active campaign in Germany: a new ELSTER phishing wave from **11 September 2026** asks for tax ID and bank details ([Verbraucherschutzforum Berlin](https://verbraucherschutzforum.berlin/2026-09-13/erneute-elster-phishing-welle-betrueger-verlangen-steuer-id-bankverbindung-und-kontaktdaten-434695/)). A request whose requester is verified against its DNS domain before any field is shown addresses that pattern directly.

The losing candidates each fail one test decisively. Change-of-address runs into an incumbent: Deutsche Post's free Umzugsmitteilung already pushes new addresses to **more than 1,000 partner companies** ([Postbank](https://www.postbank.de/privatkunden/services/vorteilsangebote/rund-um-ihren-umzug/umzug-mitteilen.html)). The unserved long tail (landlords, clubs, small suppliers) is reachable only once a network exists. A "Fill with Revoked" button reproduces the Stripe Link problem without Stripe's distribution. Link works because it ships inside Stripe Checkout, pre-loaded with **200M+ consumers** ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/payments/link)). Autofill extensions with broad host permissions also face Chrome Web Store reviews of up to several weeks ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/review-process)). Event and trip collection has the best compliance story, since a grant that expires after the trip is Art. 5(1)(e) storage limitation in code, but it serves volunteer organisers with little budget ([Stiftung Datenschutz](https://stiftungdatenschutz.org/ehrenamt/praxisratgeber/praxisratgeber-detailseite/loeschfristen-und-loeschkonzepte-277)), and it asks one-off participants to create accounts. The self-updating contact card is the most *explainable* feature: digital business cards sell "updates instantly" at $5–10/month, yet those updates reach only people who reopen the web card ([businesscards.io](https://businesscards.io/features/vcard)). It belongs in the launch post, not the business plan, because the recipient has to add a CardDAV account and the demand evidence is anecdotal.

## The primary: "Handover", signed requests in, one-click offboarding out

**User story.** Müller Webdesign in Leipzig takes on Bäckerei Schmidt for a site relaunch. Today Müller e-mails a checklist, gets a registrar password over WhatsApp and a hosting login in a PDF, and six months later nobody remembers who still holds what. With Handover, Müller creates the project "Bäckerei Schmidt – Relaunch" and ticks domain and DNS, hosting/SSH, WordPress admin and SMTP. Handover sends Schmidt a single request link. Schmidt installs the revoked app and signs up. The app checks that the request really comes from `muellerweb.de` and shows a green badge. The answer comes from records Schmidt now owns. When Schmidt's office manager changes the WordPress password in March, Müller's next read returns the new one without anyone e-mailing it. When the contract ends, Müller clicks "End engagement". Every grant goes dead, and both sides get a list of the credentials to rotate.

**What the app does.** Handover is a small, separate web app (Go plus server-rendered HTML is enough). It talks to revoked only through its public API, so it doubles as the reference integration and exposes every API gap before a third party finds it. It has four screens. The *project checklist* shows what was requested, what has been answered and what is outstanding. The *access register* shows, per client, every credential the agency holds, when it was granted and when it was last read. The *credential view* reveals the live value on demand, for staff with the right workspace permission. The *offboarding* screen revokes all grants and produces the rotation checklist. It deliberately leaves out Meta and Google ad access, which Leadsie and AgencyAccess already solve through official partner APIs. The pitch is "Leadsie for everything Leadsie can't do", not a head-on replacement.

**How it uses revoked.** The agency's workspace holds a scoped API key and a DNS-anchored identity rooted in the agency's domain. For each project, Handover creates a `requests` row from a composite template built from the shipped `domain_handover`, `server_access` and `api_credentials` templates plus a CMS-login template. It attaches the agency's identity with `identityScope = from_root`, so only an identity issued under the agency's own domain can sign the request. It sets `callbackUrl` to Handover's receiver and `maxResponses = 1`, and adds `expiresAt` when the engagement has a fixed term. The client receives `revoked://r/<slug>`. Their app probes `GET /api/public/requests/{slug}`, verifies the trust chain through the DoH TXT record `_revoked.<domain>` and the server assertion, and submits *mappings* to live vault records rather than literal data. The server then mints a `links` row in the client's workspace (invariant 12). The callback marks the checklist item as received. Values are resolved at read time through `GET /api/requests/{id}/links`, or per grant through `/s/{slug}.json`, polled with `If-None-Match` so an unchanged credential costs a 304. Offboarding flips the request to `revoked`, and the client can revoke any single link independently in their own app. An optional second deliverable is a `revoked-env` CLI or GitHub Action. It injects a client-owned API key into the agency's deploy pipeline at run time, and the client can see every read and cut it off. This is the "let your client give your pipeline their key" niche that secrets managers built for a single organisation model poorly ([dev.to](https://dev.to/ivannovazzi/how-do-you-share-secrets-with-contractors-and-ai-agents-5dh)).

**MVP scope, about six weeks for one developer.** Weeks 1–2 close the API gaps listed in the next section (API-key read of request responses, signed callbacks). Weeks 2–4 build Handover's checklist, callback receiver, access register and ETag poller against a local revoked instance. Weeks 4–5 add offboarding, the rotation checklist and a German/English UI. Week 6 goes to a one-command Docker Compose of revoked plus Handover, and to three design-partner agencies. The CLI waits until an agency asks for it.

**Why now.** Third-party access became a leading breach vector in the 2025 data, not a theoretical one. Phishing that imitates legitimate data requests is running in Germany this month. AI agents are widening the "hand a secret to a party you may later cut off" problem, as a 2026 Ask HN thread and WorkOS guidance on agent secrets show ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47736831); [WorkOS](https://workos.com/blog/ai-agent-secrets-management)). On the competitive side, 1Password's shared items are explicitly *copies*: to change one after sharing, you "update the item then share a new link" ([1Password Support](https://support.1password.com/share-items/)). "A link that stays current and that you can still revoke after they've used it" is a claim no incumbent can make.

**Getting the first users.** Price against the proven anchors: Leadsie's per-onboarding metering ($59/month for 3; [Leadsie pricing](https://www.leadsie.com/pricing)), Content Snare's per-active-request metering, and Password Pusher's $25–49/month hosted tiers ([pwpush](https://eu.pwpush.com/pricing)). Self-hosted stays free. Start with five to ten agencies from the developer's own network and the German WordPress, TYPO3 and Shopware agency communities, and treat the client's completion rate as the one metric that matters. Launch Show HN, r/selfhosted and the selfh.st newsletter inside one 48-hour window. HN exposure averages **+121 stars in 24 hours and +289 in a week** ([arXiv 2511.04453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04453)), and r/selfhosted has about 830k members ([Hive Index](https://thehiveindex.com/communities/r-selfhosted/)). Tag a release now: awesome-selfhosted lists a project only once its first release is over four months old ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/awesome-selfhosted/awesome-selfhosted-data/blob/master/CONTRIBUTING.md)). The structural payoff comes next. Every agency that onboards twenty clients creates twenty *business* revoked accounts, each with a vault that already holds company data. That is the responder network the e-invoicing alternate needs.

## Two alternates: e-invoicing data now, n8n as the channel

### E-invoicing master data that keeps itself current before 2028

**User story.** Kaiser Elektrotechnik GmbH, with €1.2M turnover, must issue EN 16931 e-invoices from **1 January 2027**. Everyone else follows on 1 January 2028 ([BMF letter 15.10.2025](https://www.bundesfinanzministerium.de/Content/DE/Downloads/BMF_Schreiben/Steuerarten/Umsatzsteuer/Umsatzsteuer-Anwendungserlass/2025-10-15-einfuehrung-obligatorische-e-rechnung.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=3); [Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.de/beitrag/e-rechnung-zweites-bmf-schreiben-sorgt-fuer-mehr-klarheit)). For each of its 300 customers it now needs something it never stored in the PDF era. That includes the **buyer electronic address (BT-49)**, which XRechnung 3.0.1 requires ([invoice-converter.com](https://www.invoice-converter.com/en/resources/xrechnung/bt-49-buyer-electronic-address)). It also needs the preferred format and channel, and for public-sector customers the Leitweg-ID, whose Peppol form is the ID with prefix `0204` ([e-rechnung-bund.de](https://e-rechnung-bund.de/en/faq/how-is-the-peppol-participant-id-connected-to-the-buyer-reference-leitweg-id/)). Kaiser sends every customer one signed "E-Rechnungsdaten" request. Each answer lands in Lexware Office as a contact update and stays current when the customer moves or switches its AP inbox.

**What it does and how it uses revoked.** A bridge service holds an API key for the supplier's workspace. It creates one request per customer from an extended `company_details` template, using `identifier` to carry the invoicing tool's contact ID. The shipped template has legal name, VAT ID, billing e-mail and address, but **no BT-49, Leitweg-ID or format field**, so extending it is the first task. On callback, and afterwards by ETag polling each grant, the bridge maps the values onto the contact. Lexware Office accepts `PUT` on contacts with optimistic `version` locking at 2 requests/second, and exposes `buyerReference` for the Leitweg-ID. Invoice Ninja's open-source v5 emits `EVENT_UPDATE_CLIENT` webhooks, which the bridge uses to detect local drift. sevDesk would need polling, since an integrator reports it publishes no webhooks ([Lexware API](https://developers.lexware.io/docs/); [Invoice Ninja source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/invoiceninja/invoiceninja/v5-stable/app/Models/Webhook.php); [Maesn](https://www.maesn.com/blog/how-to-integrate-sevdesk-api)). Any VAT ID arriving through a grant is a *claim*. The bridge should run a BZSt qualified confirmation, which matches name, town, postcode and street against the registry, and keep the result as evidence ([BZSt](https://www.bzst.de/DE/Unternehmen/Identifikationsnummern/Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer/AuslaendischeUSt-IdNr/auslaendische_ust_idnr_node.html)). Start with Invoice Ninja, whose self-hosting audience (about 10k GitHub stars) matches revoked's, and add Lexware second for reach. The MVP is about four weeks.

**Why it is not the primary.** The honest framing hurts the pitch. The BMF states that a missing BT-10 buyer reference is "umsatzsteuerlich unbeachtlich". The mandate turns bad buyer data into a machine rejection and a routing failure, not into lost input VAT, so "your VAT deduction is at risk" overstates the case. The stronger hook is fraud. **Bestellerbetrug**, where fraudsters pose as a customer to redirect deliveries, rose **61% in cases and 139% in damage** in Germany in 2025 ([Allianz Trade](https://www.allianz-trade.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/alarmstufe-ki-schaeden-durch-fake-president-betrug-vervielfachen-sich.html)), and a DNS-verified, owner-controlled address channel answers exactly that. The deeper problem is the responder. Kaiser's customer is asked to install an app and create an account to hand over data it would happily e-mail, and it benefits only once *several* suppliers ask the same way. Revocation also adds little here, because suppliers keep invoice-relevant data under their retention duties regardless. The mirror-image product avoids the account problem entirely. A supplier publishes its *own* billing data and IBAN as a living share link. Viewing is capability-based and needs no account, and customers' AP teams read it as JSON or vCard. That matters more in 2030, when ViDA makes the supplier's bank account a mandatory field on intra-EU invoices ([Invoicerr on Directive 2025/516](https://invoicerr.app/europe/vida/)). Bundle it as the "Stammdatenblatt" feature of the bridge, and sell against payment-diversion fraud rather than data freshness, because fraud has a budget line and freshness does not.

### An n8n node as the distribution layer

n8n has **206k GitHub stars**, and **13,171 npm packages** carry the `n8n-community-node-package` tag. An unverified node is installable on every self-hosted n8n the moment it is published. The verified path, which reaches n8n Cloud, has required GitHub Actions provenance since 1 May 2026 ([npm search](https://www.npmjs.com/search?q=keywords:n8n-community-node-package); [n8n docs](https://docs.n8n.io/integrations/creating-nodes/deploy/submit-community-nodes/)). revoked's primitives map onto n8n with almost no translation. A **trigger** fires on the request callback. A **"Get live value"** node reads a grant with ETag. A **"Create request"** action sends a templated ask from inside a CRM or onboarding workflow. This is a two-week build that makes both apps above reachable without custom code. For example, "when a deal closes in the CRM, send the signed billing-data request and write the answer to Lexware" becomes a workflow, not a product. Treat it as a channel with realistic expectations: tens of installs in the first months, not hundreds. A Home Assistant integration would reach a Germany-heavy base (**19.2% of 690k active installs**), but the median custom integration reports only about **20 installs** ([HA analytics](https://analytics.home-assistant.io/custom_integrations.json)), so it ranks last.

## Five API gaps block every third-party integrator today

Reading the code for this report turned up concrete blockers that any of the three apps would hit in its first week. First, `GET /api/requests/{id}/links` accepts only a `users` JWT and rejects API-key principals (`cmd/revoked/routes/requestGrants.go`). A server-side integrator such as Handover, the invoicing bridge or the n8n node cannot read responses with a scoped key. It needs a new permission key in `util.Permissions`, not a raw scope, following the project's own rule. Second, that endpoint returns at most 200 links with no pagination and no ETag. Only `/s/{slug}` supports `If-None-Match`/304 (`cmd/revoked/routes/publicShort.go`), so a requester watching 300 customers must poll grants one by one. Third, callbacks fire **once, on submission only**. They run fire-and-forget from a goroutine with a 10-second budget and are never retried, and nothing persists them across a restart (`cmd/revoked/services/callbacks.go`; the outbox is an open item in `ARCHITECTURE.md`). A responder's later update or revocation reaches the requester only as an in-app notification or on the next read. Fourth, callbacks carry only an `X-Revoked-Request` header and no signature, so the receiver cannot tell a real delivery from a forged POST and must re-fetch before trusting it. An HMAC over the body with a per-request secret closes this. Fifth, machine consumers of gated links are awkward. `/s/` refuses handshake-gated links, and CardDAV refuses all gated links. The only password unlock on `/s/` is `?password=`, which puts a secret into URLs and access logs, so the CLI path should use the `POST /api/public/links/{slug}` unlock or a dedicated scoped read instead. None of these gaps is large. All of them are the difference between "has an API" and "has integrators", which is the point of building the first app outside the core repository.

## The risks are adoption friction and overclaiming, not technology

**Two-sided adoption and the account requirement are the main threat.** Invariant 12 requires a responder account, and deep links are custom-scheme only, deliberately, so there is no web page a phisher can imitate. The client therefore installs an app *and* signs up before answering. That is sound security design and a real conversion cost. Password Pusher's "Requests" collects credentials from clients with **no account on their end** ([pwpush](https://eu.pwpush.com/requests)), and Leadsie's whole value proposition is a trivial client step. Handover survives only if the client values its access register and revocation enough to accept the friction. The design-partner phase must measure completion rate before anything is built on top. Even the state-backed EUDI wallet calls relying-party onboarding "the real bottleneck", with about 115 organisations in six months of sandbox ([Corbado](https://www.corbado.com/blog/eudi-wallet-2026-deadline-rollout-eic-2026)), which is a reminder that two-sided onboarding is hard for everyone.

**Revocation cannot unsee copies**, and credentials are the easiest thing to copy. An agency employee who pastes the live password into their own password manager has defeated the grant. The honest framing matches the EU's own: revocation stops *future* reads and makes the old copy stale, and erasure is a legal request backed by the access log. The EUDI wallet does the same, offering a dashboard and a GDPR Art. 17 erasure request rather than technical clawback ([AEPD](https://www.aepd.es/en/press-and-communication/blog/eidas2-the-eudi-wallet-and-the-gdpr-i)). That is why Handover's offboarding produces a *rotation* checklist, and why its pitch should be "revoke plus rotate", never "revoke" alone.

**Overlap with the EUDI wallet and existing tools is real but bounded.** Germany's wallet, "d-you", launches on 2 January 2027 with **identification and credential presentation only** ([Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202608/germanys-eudi-wallet-push-highlights-europes-implementation-gap)). The profitable private wallet Verimi is shutting down at the end of 2026, citing the state wallet ([heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Identity-service-provider-Verimi-ceases-business-operations-11366243.html)). revoked should never compete on identity or KYC, and should later *accept* wallet attestations, such as a verified IBAN, inside a living grant. Password Pusher, 1Password guest sharing and Leadsie each cover part of Handover's ground. None combines a templated request, a responder-owned live value and a verified requester. That combination is a positioning claim, not a moat.

**A hosted offering may trigger the Data Governance Act.** The DGA names personal data spaces and data wallets as intermediation mechanisms, and providers of data-intermediation services must notify a national authority ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/data-governance-act-explained)). The regime also imposes neutrality and structural-separation duties, which is the reading in the notes' secondary legal sources. Self-hosting, where each agency runs its own instance, probably lowers that exposure. Whether a revoked-operated SaaS for B2B credentials counts as an intermediation service is unresolved and needs legal review before a hosted tier launches. Finally, asking agencies to store clients' production credentials in a new tool from a solo developer is a trust problem. The repository's documented invariants and their regression tests are the strongest available answer, so publish them prominently rather than burying them in `CLAUDE.md`.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the intuitive order. The German e-invoicing mandate looks like the obvious wedge because it has a deadline and a legal hook. But revoked's defining mechanics, revocation and a responder who keeps control, are nearly irrelevant to invoice data that suppliers must retain anyway, and the responder has no reason to create an account. Credential handoff is where losing control is the actual harm, so it is where the account requirement becomes a selling point rather than a tax. Sequencing matters as much as selection. Handover seeds business accounts with populated company vaults, and those accounts are what later let the invoicing bridge send a request the customer can answer in one tap.

The more immediate finding concerns revoked itself. Its public API was designed for its own Flutter client. Building the first customer as a separate app, restricted to public endpoints, is the cheapest way to turn the five gaps above (API-key reads, pagination and ETags on responses, signed, retried and change-driven callbacks, and a clean machine unlock) into fixes before an outside integrator gives up on them. The success metric for the next three months should be client completion rate and whether a second agency renews, not stars.
